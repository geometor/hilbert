§ 24: Introduction of an Algebra of Segments Based Upon Desargues's Theorem and Independent of the Axioms of Congruence
=======================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: David Hilbert — *The Foundations of Geometry* (Authorized Translation by E. J. Townsend, 1902)

In order to see fully the significance of Desargues's theorem (theorem 32), let us take as the basis of our consideration a plane geometry where all of the axioms I 1–2, II–III are valid, that is to say, where all of the plane axioms of the first three groups hold, and then introduce into this geometry, in the following manner, a new algebra of segments independent of the axioms of congruence.

Take in the plane two fixed straight lines intersecting in :math:`O`, and consider only such segments as have :math:`O` for their origin and their other extremity in one of the fixed lines. We will regard the point :math:`O` itself as a segment and call it the segment :math:`O`. We will indicate this fact by writing

.. math::

   OO = 0, \quad \text{or} \quad 0 = OO.

Let :math:`E` and :math:`E'` be two definite points situated respectively upon the two fixed straight lines through :math:`O`. Then, define the two segments :math:`OE` and :math:`OE'` as the segment 1 and write accordingly

.. math::

   OE = OE' = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad 1 = OE = OE'.

We will call the straight line :math:`EE'`, for brevity, the unit-line. If, furthermore, :math:`A` and :math:`A'` are points upon

the straight lines :math:`OE` and :math:`OE'`, respectively, and, if the straight line :math:`AA'` joining them is parallel to :math:`EE'`, then we will say that the segments OA and OA' are equal to one another, and write

.. math::

   OA=OA' \quad \text{or} \quad OA'=OA.

In order now to define the sum of the segments :math:`a=OA` and :math:`b= OB`, we construct :math:`AA'` parallel to the unit-line :math:`EE'` and draw through :math:`A'` a parallel to :math:`OE` and through :math:`B` a parallel to :math:`OE'`. Let these two parallels intersect in :math:`A''`. Finally, draw through :math:`A''` a straight line parallel to the unit-line :math:`EE'`. Let this parallel cut the two fixed lines :math:`OE` and :math:`OE'` in :math:`C` and :math:`C'` respectively. Then :math:`c=OC= OC'` is called the *sum* of the segments :math:`a=OA` and :math:`b=OB`. We indicate this by writing

.. math::

   c=a+b, \quad \text{or} \quad a+b=c.

In order to define the product of a segment :math:`a=OA` by a segment :math:`b=OB`, we make use of exactly the same construction as employed in § 15, except that, in place of the sides of a right angle, we make use here of the straight lines :math:`OE` and :math:`OE'`.

The construction is consequently as follows. Determine upon :math:`OE'` a point :math:`A'` so that :math:`AA'` is parallel to the unit-line :math:`EE'`, and join :math:`E` with :math:`A'`. Then draw through :math:`B` a straight line parallel to :math:`EA'`. This parallel will intersect the fixed straight line :math:`OE'` in the point :math:`C'`, and we call :math:`c=OC'` the product of the segment :math:`a = OA` by the segment :math:`b = OB`. We indicate this relation by writing

.. math::

   c = ab, \quad \text{or} \quad ab = c.
