Chapter I: The Five Groups of Axioms — Summary
==============================================

.. rubric:: *Kapitel I: Die fünf Axiomgruppen* — David Hilbert (1899 / 1902) — §§ 1–8

Chapter I constructs the complete deductive foundation of three-dimensional Euclidean space across eight sections and **twenty foundational theorems (Theorems 1–20)**:

* **§ 1 — Primitive Systems**: Replaces :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s descriptive definitions of point, line, and plane (`I.Def.1, 4, 7`) with three unspecified systems of things bound solely by mutual relations across five axiom groups.
* **§ 2 — Group I: Connection (I 1–7, Theorems 1–2)**: Separates *planar* incidence (`I 1–2`: existence and uniqueness of the straight line joining two distinct points) from *spatial* incidence (`I 3–7`: plane determination by three non-collinear points, line-in-plane containment, two-plane intersection dimension, and existence of four non-coplanar points). Deduces unique intersection properties (`Theorem 1`) and plane determination by a line and external point or two intersecting lines (`Theorem 2`).
* **§ 3 — Group II: Order (II 1–5)**: Axiomatizes the ternary collinear relation :term:`between <zwischen>` (`II 1–4`), defines the :term:`segment <strecke>` :math:`AB`, and introduces :person:`Moritz Pasch <pasch>`'s planar triangle-piercing postulate (`II 5`, :term:`Axiom von Pasch <pasch-axiom>`).
* **§ 4 — Consequences of Order (Theorems 3–7)**: Proves unlimited linear density (`Theorem 3`), total ordering of any finite collinear point set :math:`A, B, C, D, E, \ldots, K` (`Theorem 4`), planar half-plane separation (`Theorem 5`), :term:`half-rays <halbstrahl>`, :person:`Camille Jordan <jordan>`'s polygonal separation theorem (`Theorem 6`), and spatial half-space separation (`Theorem 7`) without needing any spatial order axiom.
* **§ 5 — Group III: Parallels (Axiom III, Theorem 8)**: Adopts Playfair's uniqueness form of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s Fifth Postulate in a plane :math:`\alpha` and proves transitivity of non-intersection (`Theorem 8`). *(Note: renumbered Group IV in later editions.)*
* **§ 6 — Group IV: Congruence (IV 1–6)**: Eliminates physical motion and superposition (:term:`ἐφαρμόζειν <epharmozein>`) via six static axioms: segment transfer (`IV 1`), congruence transitivity (`IV 2`), segment additivity (`IV 3`), angle transfer (`IV 4`), angle transitivity (`IV 5`), and the triangle bridge axiom (`IV 6`) deriving base-angle congruence from two congruent sides and the included angle. *(Note: renumbered Group III in later editions.)*
* **§ 7 — Consequences of Congruence (Theorems 9–20)**: Proves congruent point-series order preservation (`Theorem 9`), SAS triangle congruence (`Theorem 10`, `Euclid I.4`), ASA congruence (`Theorem 11`), supplementary angle congruence (`Theorem 12`, `Euclid I.13`), interior/exterior angle ray decomposition (`Theorems 13–14`), **congruence of all right angles** (`Theorem 15`, proving `Euclid Postulate 4`!), SSS congruence (`Theorem 16`, `Euclid I.8`), rigid figure extension in the plane and 3-space (`Theorems 17–18`), alternate/interior angle parallelism (`Theorem 19`), and the :math:`180^\circ` triangle angle-sum theorem (`Theorem 20`).
* **§ 8 — Group V: Continuity**: Formulates the linear :term:`Axiom of Archimedes <archimedisches-axiom>` (`V`) and—in the 1900/1902 *Remark*—the metamathematical :term:`Axiom of Completeness <vollstaendigkeit>` (*Vollständigkeit*, later `V 2`).
