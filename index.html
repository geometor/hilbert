David Hilbert¶

David Hilbert (1862–1943) inaugurated modern axiomatic geometry in 1899 by stripping away unstated spatial assumptions and rebuilding Euclidean and non-Euclidean geometry from five explicit axiom groups—proving their consistency, mutual independence, and structural connection to algebraic coordinate fields. This synchronized four-track German–English edition pairs the 1899 Leipzig Festschrift with Edgar Jerome Townsend’s 1902 authorized English translation, calibrated Teubner diagrams, and cross-corpus concordances.

Key Features¶

Axiomatic Independence & Models

Explore Hilbert’s Group I–V axioms alongside explicit non-Euclidean, non-Archimedean, non-Pascalian, and non-Desarguesian independence models in Chapter II.

1899/1902 vs. 1930 Concordance

The Master Concordance maps every section and axiom-numbering shift between the 1899/1902 editions and the 7th German edition (1930), linking Euclid, Hoenen, and Pappus.
