§ 5: Group III: Axiom of Parallels (Euclid's Axiom)
===================================================

.. rubric:: David Hilbert — *The Foundations of Geometry* (Authorized Translation by E. J. Townsend, 1902)

The introduction of this axiom simplifies greatly the fundamental principles of geometry and facilitates in no small degree its development. This axiom may be expressed as follows:

III. *In a plane* :math:`\alpha` *there can be drawn through any point* :math:`A`, *lying outside of a straight line* :math:`a`, *one and only one straight line which does not intersect the line* :math:`a`. *This straight line is called the parallel to* :math:`a` *through the given point* :math:`A`.

This statement of the axiom of parallels contains two assertions. The first of these is that, in the plane :math:`\alpha`, there is always a straight line passing through :math:`A` which does not intersect the given line :math:`a`. The second states that only one such line is possible. The latter of these statements is the essential one, and it may also be expressed as follows:

.. _grundlagen-en-thm-8:

**Theorem 8.** If two straight lines :math:`a`, :math:`b` of a plane do not meet a third straight line :math:`c` of the same plane, then they do not meet each other.

For, if :math:`a`, :math:`b` had a point :math:`A` in common, there would then exist in the same plane with :math:`c` two straight lines :math:`a` and :math:`b` each passing through the point :math:`A` and not meeting the straight line :math:`c`. This condition of affairs is, however, contradictory to the second assertion contained in the axiom of parallels as originally stated. Conversely, the second part of the axiom of parallels, in its original form, follows as a consequence of theorem 8.

The axiom of parallels is a *plane axiom*.
