§ 26: The Associative Law of Multiplication and the Two Distributive Laws for the New Algebra of Segments
=========================================================================================================

.. rubric:: David Hilbert — *The Foundations of Geometry* (Authorized Translation by E. J. Townsend, 1902)

The associative law of multiplication

.. math::

   a(bc)=(ab)c

has also a place in our new algebra of segments.

Let there be given upon the first of the two fixed straight lines through :math:`O` the segments

.. math::

   1 = OA, \quad b = OC, \quad c=OA'

and upon the second of these straight lines, the segments

.. math::

   a=OG,\quad b=OB.

In order to construct the segments

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   bc &= OB',\ \text{and} \quad \ bc=OC', \\
   ab &= OD, \\
   (ab) c &= OD',
   \end{aligned}

in accordance with § 24, draw :math:`A'B'` parallel to :math:`AB`, :math:`B'C'` parallel to :math:`BC`, :math:`CD` parallel to :math:`AG`, and :math:`A'D'` parallel to :math:`AD`. We see at once that the given law amounts to the same as saying that :math:`CD` must also be parallel to :math:`C'D'`. Denote the point of intersection of the straight lines :math:`A'D'` and :math:`B'C'` by :math:`F'` and that of the straight lines :math:`AD` and :math:`BC` by :math:`F`. Then the triangles :math:`ABF` and :math:`A'B' F'` have their homologous sides parallel to each other, and, according to Desargues's theorem, the three points :math:`O`, :math:`F`, :math:`F'` must lie in a straight line. Because of these conditions, we can apply the second part of Desargues's theorem to the two triangle :math:`CDF` and :math:`C' D'F'`, and hence show that, in fact, :math:`CD` is parallel to :math:`C'D'`.

Finally, upon the basis of Desargues's theorem, we shall show that the two distributive laws

.. math::

   a (b+c) = ab+ac

and

.. math::

   (a+b)c = ac+bc

hold for our algebra of segments.

In the proof of the first one of these laws, we shall make use of figure 46. [#fn1]_ In this figure, we have

.. math::

   \begin{array}{c}
   b = OA', c=OC', \\
   ab=OB', ab=OA'', ac=OC'', etc.
   \end{array}

In the same figure, :math:`B'' D_2` is parallel to :math:`C''D_1` which is parallel to the fixed straight line :math:`OA'`, and :math:`B'D_1` is parallel to :math:`C'D_2`, which is parallel to the fixed straight line :math:`OA''`. Moreover, we have :math:`A' A''` parallel to :math:`C'C''`, and :math:`A'B''` parallel to :math:`B' A''`, parallel to :math:`F'D_2`, parallel to :math:`F''D_1`.

Our proposition amounts to asserting that we must necessarily have also

:math:`F'F''` parallel to :math:`A'A''` and to :math:`C'C''`.

We construct the following auxiliary lines:

| :math:`F''J` parallel to the fixed straight line :math:`OA'`,
| :math:`F'J` parallel to the fixed straight line :math:`OA''`.

Let us denote the points of intersection of the straight lines :math:`C''D_1` and :math:`C'D_2`, :math:`C''D_1` and :math:`F'J`, :math:`C'D_2` and :math:`F''J` by :math:`G`, :math:`H_1`, :math:`H_2`, respectively. Finally, we obtain the other auxiliary lines indicated in the figure by joining the points already constructed.

In the two triangles :math:`A' B'' C''` and :math:`F'D_2G`, the straight lines joining homologous vertices are parallel to each other. According to the second part of Desargues's theorem, it follows, therefore, that

:math:`A'C''` is parallel to :math:`F' G`.

In the two triangles :math:`A'C''F''` and :math:`F'GH_2`, the straight lines joining the homologous vertices are also parallel to each other. From the properties already demonstrated, it follows by virtue of the second part of Desargues's theorem that we must have

:math:`A'F''` parallel to :math:`F'H_2`.

Since in the two horizontally shaded triangles :math:`OA'F'` and :math:`JH_2F'` the homologous sides are parallel, Desargues's theorem shows that the three straight lines joining the homologous vertices, viz.:

.. math::

   OJ,\ A'H_2,\ F''F'

all intersect in one and the same point, say in :math:`P`.

In the same way, we have necessarily

.. math::

   A''F' \; \text{parallel to} \; F''H_1

and since, in the two obliquely shaded triangles :math:`OA''F'` and :math:`JH_1F''`, the homologous sides are parallel, then, in consequence of Desargues's theorem, the three straight lines joining the homologous vertices, viz.:

.. math::

   OJ, A''H_1, F'F''

all intersect likewise in the same point, namely, in point :math:`P`.

Moreover, in the triangles :math:`OA'A''` and :math:`JH_2H_1`, the straight lines joining the homologous vertices all pass through this same point :math:`P`, and, consequently, it follows that we have

.. math::

   H_1H_2 \; \text{parallel to} \; A'A'',

and, therefore,

.. math::

   H_1H_2 \; \text{parallel to} \; C'C'',

Finally, let us consider the figure :math:`F''H_2C'C''H_1F'F''`. Since, in this figure, we have

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   F''H_2 \; \text{parallel to} \; &\; C'F', \; \text{parallel to} \; C''H_1, \\
   GH_2C' \quad \text{" "}\quad &\; F''C'', \; \text{" "}\quad H_1F', \\
   C'C'' \quad \text{" "}\quad &\; H_1H_2, \;
   \end{aligned}

we recognize here again figure 43, which we have already made use of in § 25 to prove the commutative law of addition. The conclusions, analogous to those which we reached there, show that we must have

.. math::

   F'F'' \; \text{parallel to} \; H_1H_2

and, consequently, we must have also

.. math::

   F'F'' \; \text{parallel to} \; A'A'',

which result concludes our demonstration.

To prove the second formula of the distributive law, we make use of an entirely different figure,–figure 47. In this figure, we have

.. math::

   1 = OD,\ a = OA,\ a = OB,\ b = OG,\ c = OD',

.. math::

   ac = OA',\ ac = OB',\ BC = OG', \; \text{etc.} ,

and, furthermore, we have

| :math:`GH` parallel to :math:`G'H'`, parallel to the fixed line :math:`OA`,
| :math:`GH` parallel to :math:`A'H'`, parallel to the fixed line :math:`OB`,

We have also

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   AB &\; \text{parallel to} \; A'B' \\
   BD &\; \text{" "} \; B'D' \\
   DG &\; \text{" "} \; D'G' \\
   HJ &\; \text{" "} \; H'J'.
   \end{aligned}

That which we are to prove amounts, then, to demonstrating that

.. math::

   DJ \; \text{must be parallel to} \; D'J'.

Denote the points in which :math:`BD` and :math:`GD` intersect the straight line :math:`AH` by :math:`C` and :math:`F`, respectively, and the points in which :math:`B'D'` and :math:`G'D'` intersect the straight line :math:`A'H'` by :math:`C'` and :math:`F'`, respectively. Finally, draw the auxiliary lines :math:`FJ` and :math:`F'J'`, indicated in the figure by dotted lines.

In the triangles :math:`ABC` and :math:`A'B'C'`, the homologous sides are parallel and, consequently, by Desargues's theorem the three points :math:`O`, :math:`C`, :math:`C'` lie on a straight line. Then, by considering in the same way the triangles :math:`CDF` and :math:`CD'F'`, it follows that the points :math:`O`, :math:`F`, :math:`F'` lie upon the same straight line and likewise, from a consideration of the triangles :math:`FGH` and :math:`F'G'H'`, we find the points :math:`O`, :math:`H`, :math:`H'` to be situated on a straight line. Now, in the triangles :math:`FHJ` and :math:`F'H'J'`, the straight lines joining the homologous vertices all pass through the same point :math:`O`, and, hence, as a consequence of the second part of Desargues's theorem, the straight lines :math:`FJ` and :math:`F'J'` must also be parallel to each other. Finally, a consideration of the triangles :math:`DFJ` and :math:`D'F'J'` shows that the straight lines :math:`DJ` and :math:`D'J'` are parallel to each other and with this our proof is completed.

.. [#fn1] Figures 46, 47, and 48 were designed by Dr. Von Schaper, as have also the details of the demonstrations relating to these figures.
