Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge & Repurposing It for Exact Symbolic Reasoning

About This Edition¶

GEOMETOR hilbert reconstructs the axiomatic and metamathematical keystone of modern geometry: David Hilbert’s landmark treatise Grundlagen der Geometrie (The Foundations of Geometry). Published in June 1899 as Part I of the Festschrift zur Feier der Enthüllung des Gauss-Weber-Denkmals in Göttingen by B. G. Teubner, and authorized for English translation by Edgar Jerome Townsend in 1902 (The Open Court Publishing Company), Hilbert’s work established the modern axiomatic method. By detaching geometric entities from sensory intuition and establishing formal relation systems across five rigorous axiom groups (incidence, betweenness, congruence, parallels, and continuity), Hilbert demonstrated the independence, consistency, and structural limits of geometric deduction.

Every definition, axiom, theorem (§§ 1–39, Theorems 1–44), and independence model has been extracted into structured, machine-addressable semantic elements. The edition presents a synchronized four-track Book Codex aligning the 1899 German editio princeps with Townsend’s 1902 authorized translation (including Hilbert’s 1900 completeness axiom Vollständigkeitsaxiom, Max Dehn’s non-Legendrian analysis, and the 1902 transformation group appendix). Geometric figures from the original Teubner edition have been reconstructed as calibrated, resolution-independent vector graphics, and Hilbert’s segment arithmetic (Streckenrechnung) and algebraic number fields (\(\Omega\), \(\Omega(t)\)) are linked directly to exact symbolic algebra.

Within the GEOMETOR canon, hilbert serves as the critical meta-axiomatic horizon. It provides the logical framework underpinning Euclid's Elements (geometor.elements and geometor.euclid), links to the projective Pappus-Desargues configurations of geometor.pappus, and interfaces directly with the epistemological critique of Fr. Peter Hoenen, S.J. (De Noetica Geometriae, 1954). Through the computational infrastructure of GEOMETOR, Hilbert’s minimal countable Pythagorean coordinate fields and segment calculi become living, inspectable algebraic systems.

The GEOMETOR Mission¶

Founded and directed by phi ARCHITECT, the GEOMETOR initiative is dedicated to the systematic digitization, symbolic formalization, and multi-medium re-articulation of classical geometric and axiomatic literature across 2,300+ years of mathematical history. GEOMETOR bridges historical philology and modern computational geometry through three structural pillars:

Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge

Moving beyond passive page scans and lossy optical character recognition (OCR), GEOMETOR recovers the living deductive architecture of classical, Renaissance, and modern mathematical treatises. Enunciations, geometric constructions, proof steps, axiomatic justifications, marginal citations, primary-source folios (rendered dynamically through publish folio), and calibrated Ink-to-Vector .euclid-graphic diagrams are converted into structured, machine-addressable semantic graphs.

Exact Symbolic Grounding (geometor.model)

Eliminating floating-point approximation errors entirely, GEOMETOR anchors geometric constructions and center proofs in 100% exact symbolic algebra via sympy and Rational Trigonometry invariants (quadrance, spread, cross-ratio) evaluated over exact algebraic number fields. Every center, line concurrence, circle intersection, and tangency condition is verified to exact symbolic zero.

Repurposing Knowledge Across Mediums

Extracted semantic and algebraic models are mobilized across synchronized multi-track Book Codices and EPUB 3 editions via photon-platform/publish, computable deductive canons (geometor.elements, geometor.etc, geometor.pappus, geometor.divine), interactive SVG laboratories (geometor.explorer), high-definition visual broadcasts, and neuro-symbolic program synthesis engines (geometor.seer) across geometor.com.

Sources, Provenance & Methodology¶

This edition is established upon verified primary textual witnesses and systematic philological collation:

Primary Textual Witnesses

  • 1899 German Editio Princeps (B. G. Teubner, Leipzig): Published in June 1899 as Part I (Erster Theil, pp. 1–92) of the Festschrift zur Feier der Enthüllung des Gauss-Weber-Denkmals in Göttingen, issued by the Fest-Comitee (together with Emil Wiechert’s Grundlagen der Elektrodynamik). Archived verbatim from Internet Archive identifier grundlagendergeo00hilb and verified by SHA-256 checksum in resources/grundlagen/sources_manifest.json. (Philological note: Project Gutenberg eBook ``#22062`` is John Dee’s 1570 *Mathematicall Praeface to Euclid, not Hilbert’s German text.)*

  • 1902 Authorized English Translation (Open Court, Chicago): Translated by Edgar Jerome Townsend, Ph.D. (University of Illinois), incorporating Hilbert’s additions from L. Laugel’s 1900 French edition (Les principes fondamentaux de la géométrie, Gauthier-Villars) and Hilbert’s 1902 Mathematische Annalen memoir on transformation groups. Archived verbatim from Project Gutenberg eBook #17384.

  • 1930 Seventh German Edition Concordance: Because Fr. Peter Hoenen, S.J. (De Noetica Geometriae, 1954) cites Hilbert’s revised 7th edition (1930)—in which Groups III and IV are swapped, Axiom II 4 is demoted to Satz 5 after E. H. Moore’s 1902 redundancy proof, flächengleich is renamed zerlegungsgleich, and Streckenübertrager is specialized to Eichmass—every shift is explicitly mapped in the Master Concordance and unit Notes.

Digital Scriptorium Methodology

  • Four-Track Synchronized Codex: Readers can inspect each section across parallel tracks: Townsend 1902 English translation, Teubner 1899 German text, chapter synopses, and scholarly commentary.

  • Scholarly & Cross-Repository Apparatus: For full bibliographical citations, see Bibliography; for biographical notes on figures cited throughout the treatise, see People; for mathematical and philological terminology, see Glossary; and for cross-treatise mapping with Euclid, Hoenen, and Pappus, see Concordance.
