Architectural Summary of the Conclusion & Appendix¶

  • Purity of Method (*Reinheit der Methode*): In the Schlusswort (Conclusion), Hilbert distills the philosophical moral of the entire treatise: mathematics is satisfied only when either a rigorous proof is achieved or the reason for the impossibility of a proof under prescribed means is demonstrated—linking geometric independence proofs directly to Abel’s quintic impossibility theorem and Hermite–Lindemann’s transcendence of $e$ and $\pi$, and grounding the demand for purity of method in exact axiomatic dependency analysis.

  • Max Dehn’s 1900 Additions (Townsend 1902 Conclusion): Incorporates Max Dehn’s two 1900 Göttingen breakthroughs: 1. Non-Legendrian & Semi-Euclidean Geometries (Dehn 1900): analyzing triangle angle-sum vs. parallel multiplicity when Archimedes’s Axiom is omitted (producing the famous $3 \times 3$ table showing elliptic/angle-sum $> 2R$ coexisting with infinitely many parallels once Archimedes is dropped!). 2. Polyhedral Volume / Hilbert’s Third Problem (Dehn 1900): proving that, unlike planar polygons (Chapter IV), three-dimensional tetrahedra of equal base and equal altitude cannot in general be made flächengleich (equidecomposable) or even inhaltsgleich (equicomplementable), even with Archimedes’s Axiom!

  • Townsend’s 1902 Appendix — Topological Transformation Groups: Summarizes Hilbert’s 1902 Mathematische Annalen memoir (Hilbert 1902), re-founding plane Euclidean and Bolyai–Lobachevsky geometry from Sophus Lie’s transformation-group standpoint without assuming differentiability, using Georg Cantor’s point-set topology, Camille Jordan’s closed curves, and three topological group axioms (Axioms I–III)—the inaugural step toward solving Hilbert’s Fifth Problem.
