1. Heath on Euclid I.35 and Hilbert’s Edition Shift (flächengleich → zerlegungsgleich)¶

In geometor/euclid (volume_I/book_i/propositions/35/commentary.rst, folio 0342, Heath 1908, Vol. I, p. 328), T. L. Heath devotes a special section to Hilbert’s Chapter IV distinctions. Readers comparing the 1899 German Festschrift, Townsend’s 1902 English, and Heath’s 1908 commentary should note an essential terminological evolution:

Why does Euclid I.35 (“Parallelograms which are on the same base and in the same parallels are equal to one another”) work without Archimedes’s Axiom only for inhaltsgleich and NOT for flächengleich? Look at fig_polygon_equivalence.svg (§ 19): when parallelogram $AB'C'D$ is sheared arbitrarily far to the right—say by the non-Archimedean distance $AF = t$ in $\Omega(t)$ while $AD = 1$—one can still add the single triangle $AED$ and subtract congruent trapezoids in one step (inhaltsgleich), whereas dissecting one triangle into congruent pieces to tile the sheared triangle would require $\ge t$ steps, which no finite integer $n$ can reach! With Archimedes’s Axiom restored, Farkas Bolyai and Paul Gerwien (1833) proved that inhaltsgleich and zerlegungsgleich coincide in the plane.

2. Proving Euclid’s Common Notion 5 (De Zolt’s Postulate, §§ 19–21)¶

In § 19, Hilbert quotes Euclid’s Greek text of Common Notion 5 verbatim:

„Καὶ τὸ ὅλον τοῦ μέρους μεῖζόν ἐστιν“ (“And the whole is greater than the part”)

pointing out that when Euclid invokes C.N. 5 in I.39 to prove equal-area triangles on the same base have equal altitude (Theorem 28), it is not a purely logical truism—without a proof that polygonal area cannot remain unchanged when a piece is removed (Antonio De Zolt’s 1881 postulate, De Zolt 1881), every polygon could trivially be inhaltsgleich to every other! By constructing the segment-valued area functional $F(P) \in \Omega_{\text{seg}}$ in § 20 via Chapter III’s Pappus-based Streckenrechnung and proving triangulation invariance (Theorem 29), Hilbert converts Euclid’s Common Notion 5 into a rigorous theorem (`Theorem 31`)!
