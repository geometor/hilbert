GEOMETOR Commentary & Cross-Repository Notes¶

1. Dimensionality and the Meaning of Spatial Incidence (§§ 22–23, 30)¶

Why can Desargues’s Theorem (Theorem 32) be proved in three lines in 3D projective/affine space using only incidence axioms (I 1–7, II, III), yet cannot be proved at all in 2D planar incidence-order-parallel geometry (I 1–2, II, III, V)?

  • In 3-Space: Given two perspective triangles in plane $\alpha$, one lifts the perspective rays into a triangular pyramid in 3-space so corresponding sides lie in two planes $\beta, \gamma$; the intersection line $\beta \cap \gamma$ (guaranteed by spatial Axiom I 6!) meets $\alpha$ in the Desargues axis (or parallel direction).

  • In the Plane Alone (`§ 23`, `Theorem 33`): Without a third spatial dimension (I 3–7) or planar triangle congruence (IV 6), Hilbert’s refracted ellipse model bends straight lines locally inside $x^2 + 4y^2 = 1$ along circular arcs through $F(\tfrac{3}{2}, 0)$, destroying perspective concurrency while preserving all planar incidence, order, parallel, and continuity axioms! (Historical note: in 1902 Forest Ray Moulton simplified Hilbert’s ellipse construction to refracted broken lines across the $X$-axis—the “Moulton plane”—after Hilbert’s 1899 breakthrough showed such planes exist.)

  • Elimination of Spatial Axioms (`§ 30`, `Theorem 35`): As Hilbert summarizes memorably in § 30, Desargues’s Theorem is “the result of the elimination of the space axioms” for planar geometry, and simultaneously unlocks embedding into projective/affine spaces of arbitrary dimension $n$!

2. Non-Commutative Geometry Before Quantum Mechanics (§§ 24–29)¶

Notice the astonishing modernity of §§ 24–29: twenty-six years before Heisenberg, Born, and Jordan introduced non-commutative matrix mechanics in Göttingen (1925), Hilbert constructs coordinate geometry over a non-commutative division ring (skew-field, Desarguessches Zahlensystem). In Theorem 34 (§ 27), left-multiplication vs. right-multiplication becomes geometrically decisive:

  • $ax + by + c = 0$ (coefficients on the left) defines a straight line;

  • $xa + yb + c = 0$ (coefficients on the right) in general does not define a straight line unless multiplication commutes—i.e., unless Pappus’s/Pascal’s Theorem holds!
